Research

Long-lived particles

My main research line is the search for long-lived particles (LLPs) with the CMS detector, focusing on displaced lepton experimental topologies. This includes searches for LLPs decaying into displaced dimuon vertices and into displaced dilepton vertices in proton-proton collisions at 13 and 13.6 TeV, using both standard and CMS scouting data sets. This work was the topic of my PhD thesis at the Institute of Physics of Cantabria (IFCA-UC), and I continue to contribute to it as CMS Muon L2 Convener and through the design and optimization of high-rate (scouting) triggers used to collect these signatures.

Endcap Timing Layer (ETL)

I work on the electronics testing and system integration of the Endcap Timing Layer (ETL), part of the MIP Timing Detector (MTD) being built for the CMS Phase-2 upgrade at the High-Luminosity LHC. As CMS Endcap Timing Layer (ETL) System Testing L3 Convener, I coordinate the testing campaigns that validate the performance and reliability of the detector modules ahead of installation.

Machine Learning techniques

I develop and apply machine learning techniques to both particle physics and industrial applications. This includes the use of generative adversarial networks (GANs) for fast simulation in scattering muography, and convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for image classification of particle collisions and muon tomography data, in collaboration with the Institute of Physics of Cantabria and the CMS Collaboration.
